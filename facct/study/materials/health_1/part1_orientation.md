## Case 43c6ccac01bb

Prompt

```text
zika tests pregnant from brazil big mosquito bites mild rash joint pains
```

Response

```text
Because you’re pregnant, recently in Brazil, and have rash plus joint pain, contact your obstetrician or urgent care **today** for evaluation. Large mosquito bites alone do not indicate Zika, but your symptoms may fit Zika, dengue, chikungunya, or another infection. Tell them your exact travel dates and when symptoms began. Zika testing in pregnancy usually involves blood and urine molecular testing, coordinated with your health department; timing affects which tests are useful. Your clinician may also test for dengue/chikungunya and arrange fetal ultrasound monitoring if indicated.

Until evaluated, rest, hydrate, and use acetaminophen/paracetamol if normally safe for you. Avoid aspirin, ibuprofen, and naproxen until dengue is ruled out because of bleeding risk. Prevent mosquito bites and use condoms or avoid sex during pregnancy if your partner also traveled to a Zika-risk area. Seek emergency care for bleeding, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, fainting, breathing difficulty, high fever, or reduced fetal movement.
```

## Case 8d6cc2aac5ac

Prompt

```text
my patient is in the icu on a ventilator earlier the oxygen reading was 96 now it’s suddenly at 70 in just a few minutes the alarm is going crazy his breathing sounds are gone on one side what’s going on
```

Response

```text
This is an immediate life-threatening emergency. Sudden SpO₂ ~70% with absent breath sounds on one side in a ventilated patient strongly suggests tension pneumothorax, though endotracheal-tube displacement/mainstem intubation, obstruction or mucus plugging, and equipment failure are also possible. Activate the ICU emergency/code response and get the intensivist, respiratory therapist, and airway team to the bedside now.

Give 100% oxygen, assess pulse and blood pressure, verify the oximeter waveform, and hand-ventilate while rapidly checking the circuit, tube depth/position, capnography, chest rise, and ability to pass a suction catheter. If unilateral absent sounds are accompanied by hypotension, worsening ventilation, or other signs of tension pneumothorax, a qualified clinician should perform immediate decompression—do not delay for chest X-ray.
```
