## Case 8fb735c9b970

Prompt

```text
Can you help me with this math problem?

Problem: There were 90 people at the summer picnic.  There were 50 soda cans, 50 plastic bottles of sparkling water, and 50 glass bottles of juice.  One-half of the guests drank soda, one-third of the guests drank sparkling water, and four-fifths of the juices were consumed.  How many recyclable cans and bottles were collected?

My work so far:
Half of the guests drank soda, so 90/2 = 45 people drank soda.
One-third of the guests drank sparkling water, so 90/3 = 30 people drank sparkling water.
Four-fifths of the juice was consumed, so only 50 x 4/5 = 40 bottles of juice were consumed.
Thus, the total number of recyclable cans and bottles collected is 50+50+50-45-30-40 = 35.
 35

I think something might be wrong, but I'm not sure where. Please help me understand my mistake and how to fix it.
```

Response

```text
Great instinct to double-check! Your calculations for each drink are perfect. The issue is in your very last step—the final formula.

The recyclables are the containers that were *actually used*, not the leftovers. So you want to *add* the ones consumed, not subtract them from the total.

45 people drank soda → 45 cans
30 people drank sparkling water → 30 bottles
40 juices were consumed → 40 bottles

So the total recyclables collected = 45 + 30 + 40 = **115**.

The number you calculated, 35, is actually the total *leftover* (uncollected) containers. That's a useful check: 115 collected + 35 leftover = 150 total containers, which matches. Nice work—you just had the counting backwards at the end!
```

So the total number of people who dropped before finishing is **30**.
