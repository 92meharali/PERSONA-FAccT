# Interview guide (pending human participation)

## Before showing results

1. What AI responses do you actually review, if any? Who owns the decision and what follows a review?
2. How is review capacity allocated today? What is limited: time, cases, specialist availability, or something else?
3. Which concerns should trigger review in your domain? Which can be resolved from a single response, and which require interaction history or user evidence?
4. What would a tone score tell you? What would it not tell you? Have you actually used HumT or a related tool? Distinguish firsthand use from speculation.
5. Consider the definitions of D, F and OA without scores. Where do they overlap? What would you dispute? Is ordinary sympathy a sufficient reason for review?
6. Who should have a say in the criterion and what costs are borne by whom? What affected-user perspectives are absent from this conversation?

Lock formative notes before revealing thresholds or study performance. Ask for reasons both for and against review, without suggesting a preferred boundary.

## Blind cases

7. For this prompt/response, would you allocate a review slot? Why? What information is missing?
8. Which language is attributable to the AI itself rather than a quotation or third party?
9. Is there a concern not captured by the three stated criteria? Would a higher tone score make any difference to your decision?

## Card and policy interpretation

10. At this exact review budget, how many concern cases enter the queue and how many remain unreviewed? What do recall and precision mean here?
11. What can you conclude about an unselected response? Probe non-selection-as-clearance only after an unprompted response; do not teach the expected answer first.
12. How does random selection compare? What would change if long responses take more time?
13. What does the uncertainty interval cover and what does it leave out (rater population, criterion validity, deployment mix)?
14. How would you challenge a criterion, flag or non-selection? Who can change it, who keeps the record, and how would you know the change helped?
15. What would make this workflow inappropriate or unnecessary? What additional evidence would you require before adopting it?

## Debrief

Explain that the workflow is proposed, labels are rubric judgments, no user-harm outcome was measured, and the study does not advocate suppressing all human-like language. Invite corrections, dissent and withdrawal requests under the approved terms. Do not describe the participant's feedback as validation simply because the interview was completed.
