# Contribution and scope

## Current assessment addition: 10 October 2026

The paper now evaluates unchanged mental-health queues against new contextual
reviewworthiness judgments. R1/R2 assess 660 responses each; R3 assesses only
14 disagreements. The 19 resolved positives give HumT coverage 6 versus markers'
12 at 132 slots, with paired intervals crossing zero. Ordinary sympathy/pronouns
do not automatically meet this target, but lexical association can still explain
baseline performance. Different capability-note versions and sparse support are
retained, not corrected away. This adds judgment evidence beyond the original
source-rubric analysis, not a new corpus or validated organizational workflow.

RQ1 includes this follow-up; RQ2 and RQ3 retain their original computational
meaning. Participant backgrounds, recruitment/payment details and the applicable
approval reference are still deferred despite author-confirmed separation and
prior consent/coverage. The rest of this brief records the computational core;
its original no-new-judgments scope is superseded only by this explicit addition.

## Decision

A model-evaluation team has a defined batch of generated prompt-response pairs and
can inspect only a fraction. It considers recorded HumT as a ranking rule because
its review purpose includes anthropomorphic language. This is a proposed workflow,
not established adoption. Selection affords review; non-selection is not clearance.
Review actions, reading time, and downstream harm reduction are not observed.

## Three empirical questions

1. At matched item capacities, does HumT capture rubric-defined concerns better than
   expected random selection, length, and a transparent marker score?
2. Does adding HumT improve a fixed marker/log-length logistic screen on held-out
   prompt groups? This is not a comparison with the best semantic evaluator.
3. What coverage differences become visible when categories compete for the same
   domain-wide queue, rather than receiving separately reranked quotas?

## What the reader learns

The contribution is the corpus-specific answer to these questions, not invention
of recall, capacity constraints, measurement-use validity, or category auditing.
Enrichment against random, complementarity of selected sets, incremental gain in a
trained screen, and distribution of residual concerns are different quantities.
The original CHI OA-prediction coefficients cannot supply these operating points or
the comparative queues. Nevertheless, all observations and substantial motivation
are reused; this is not an independent replication of CHI.

## Main findings and limits

- HumT D recall at 20% is 42/165, 7/12, 10/23 across domains. Markers catch 63, 9, 21.
  Comparisons for F/OA differ; no universal safety-policy ranking follows.
- All nine trained incremental intervals include zero. This is model-, budget-,
  partition- and criterion-specific uncertainty, not equivalence or redundancy.
- General-health markers catch 32/120 fit cases overall versus HumT's 25/120, but
  4/74 versus 8/74 among natural prompts. The natural contrast is uncertain
  (+5.4 pp for H, 95% interval -1.3 to +13.0). Do not promote it to proven reversal.
- Workload and the worked reporting card interpret these same rankings; neither
  is independent empirical validation. The card is an unevaluated proposal.

## Comparator decision

Retain the transparent fixed and trained policies. No extra judge, classifier
search, equivalence margin, or threshold optimization. If later claims require
competitiveness with modern semantic screens, this specification must change and
that experiment must be justified separately. More models cannot validate the target.

## Publication decision

Suitable for internal review as a bounded computational audit. Practical relevance,
sufficient independent scholarly value, and concurrent-submission eligibility are
not certified by this framing or by test success. The author handles confidential
venue guidance. No participant study or future independent data is promised.
