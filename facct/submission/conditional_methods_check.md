# Conditional methods check

Checked 2 October 2026 against primary scikit-learn 1.8 documentation. This reviews the supplied plan, not the implementation or results. It supports an exploratory conditional comparison, not confirmatory evaluation. Statistical implications below are deductions from the specified design, not coverage guarantees supplied by scikit-learn.

## Verified software behavior

- **Grouped CV:** `GroupKFold` keeps groups disjoint between training and test and tests each group once; five folds require at least five distinct prompts per domain. `shuffle=True` with an integer `random_state` permits reproducible randomized grouping; the seed has no effect without shuffling. Freeze a label-independent prompt-to-fold map within each domain, shared across outcomes and policies. Do not substitute class-stratified splitting or merely pass `cv=5`. A custom frozen map need not reproduce GroupKFold's exact assignments. [GroupKFold 1.8](https://scikit-learn.org/1.8/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.GroupKFold.html)
- **Preprocessing:** `StandardScaler` learns feature means and scales at fit time and reuses them on transform. Fit separately on each training subset, including every bootstrap refit; only transform the held-out batch. Label-free fitting on the entire corpus is still preprocessing leakage. A training-fitted pipeline is the documented safeguard. [StandardScaler 1.8](https://scikit-learn.org/1.8/modules/generated/sklearn.preprocessing.StandardScaler.html), [leakage guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/1.8/common_pitfalls.html#data-leakage)
- **Logistic defaults:** effective L2 regularization, `C=1.0`, `solver='lbfgs'`, `class_weight=None`, and an intercept are confirmed. In 1.8, `penalty` is deprecated and the signature uses a deprecation sentinel; `l1_ratio=0.0` expresses L2. State effective settings rather than copying an older signature. Default `max_iter=100` does not establish convergence: record tolerance, iteration limit and warnings. A prespecified convergence allowance is not outcome-based tuning. [LogisticRegression 1.8](https://scikit-learn.org/1.8/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression.html)

## Estimand and resampling contract

Reduced features are `[marker fraction, log1p(word count)]`; extended features add `H`. Keep transformations, outcome definitions, budgets, score direction and label-independent tie handling fixed, with no tuning. For each held-out batch `j`, every policy selects exactly `k_j = floor(b * n_j)`. Report `sum_j catches_j / sum_j P_j`, not the unweighted mean of fold recalls. Pool counts, not scores from separately fitted models: the latter creates a different allocation rule. The documentation likewise warns that pooled cross-validated predictions come from distinct models and are not interchangeable with fold evaluation. [CV prediction guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/1.8/modules/cross_validation.html#obtaining-predictions-by-cross-validation)

For each of 2,000 paired draws, resample whole prompts with replacement within each original domain-by-fold stratum, retaining its original prompt count and all associated rows. Use the same sampled clusters for both models and all comparator policies. For each held-out stratum, refit both scalers/models on the other four resampled strata, then rerank its resampled batch and recompute its capacity. Duplicate prompt copies retain their original fold; retain multiplicities in training, evaluation and counts. Do not redraw folds or independently resample each model/fold's training set. Compute the within-draw policy difference before taking interval endpoints.

Describe intervals as **conditional prompt-cluster bootstrap intervals given the original partition and empirical labeled corpus**, with the interval construction and nominal level stated. Observed labels travel with sampled prompts; this does not fix each draw's prevalence or positive count. Resampling the labels is not modeling annotation uncertainty. Two thousand draws controls simulation effort, not exact or validated coverage.

## Serious caveats and required reporting

1. **Sparse support and ranking discontinuities:** few positive-bearing prompts, tied scores, and integer cutoffs can produce discrete or unstable bootstrap differences. Single-class training uses the declared constant training-prevalence fallback, flagged and counted, not a successful logistic fit. Its tied ranking must use the same label-independent rule as other policies. Zero-positive recall is `NaN`; a zero-positive fold still contributes its counts to the aggregate, which is defined if total `P > 0`. Report undefined-draw and finite-paired-draw counts; dropping undefined draws conditions the interval further. Do not silently treat convergence failures as single-class fallbacks.
2. **Conditional support is narrower than deployment:** prompt-cluster resampling assumes prompts are suitable exchangeable units within strata. It does not capture dependence across prompts from shared templates, raters or generators, nor new-domain or new-rater uncertainty. Grouped CV protects only the grouping actually supplied. [Grouped-data rationale](https://scikit-learn.org/1.8/modules/cross_validation.html#cross-validation-iterators-for-grouped-data)
3. **Batch and training-size dependence:** five-fold training uses four strata, not the final full-data fit. Local quotas can select different cases from whole-domain ranking; `sum floor(b*n_j)` can be smaller than `floor(b*sum n_j)`. Recompute label-free comparators under these same batches rather than reuse full-batch results. Report realized capacities and positive denominators. Unequal cluster sizes also make this response-level recall, not equal-prompt recall.
4. **Limited interpretation of adding H:** the difference measures incremental ranking utility for this fixed regularized additive-logit specification. It is neither a causal effect nor a general conditional-independence test; gains could reflect functional-form limitations in the reduced model. No tuning does not erase earlier label-informed analytic choices. Treat intervals across outcomes/budgets as exploratory, not simultaneous evidence.
5. **Split sensitivity:** report all ten prespecified alternative seeds, their effects and degeneracy flags. These reuse the same corpus and are neither independent replications nor opportunities for best-seed selection. They expose partition dependence but do not add partition uncertainty to the original conditional interval.

## Distinction and evidence still missing

The learned policies consume task-specific empirical labels to estimate coefficients; label-free policies do not fit to those labels, although H itself can be model-derived. A shared budget matches downstream review slots, not annotation, training or H-acquisition costs. Adding H tests its incremental value beyond these two features for this learner; it does not establish superiority of a label-free H policy or a cost-effective deployment.

Still needed from the implementation/results: the frozen fold map and seeds; group-disjointness checks; feature/label alignment and availability; training-only preprocessing checks; convergence/fallback logs; fold and positive-cluster counts; realized quotas/ties; finite bootstrap counts and paired intervals; and the complete ten-seed sensitivity summary. Not established by this analysis: annotation validity or uncertainty, bootstrap coverage for this exact procedure, untouched external generalization, acquisition-cost advantage, or actual reviewer benefit. No additional authority or generic novelty claim is needed to state these limits.
