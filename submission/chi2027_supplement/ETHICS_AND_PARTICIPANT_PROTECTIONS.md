# Ethics and participant protections

The annotation study was conducted as a sub-study under a broader research
project approved by the authors' university Ethical Institutional Review Board
on 12 February 2026. The approval record names the parent project rather than
PERSONA. Institutional, investigator, grant, and protocol-identifying details
are withheld during anonymous review.

## Participants and separation

The study involved 20 raters in non-overlapping pools:

- three domain-specific Group A pools of five raters each provided holistic
  overall-appropriateness (`OA`) judgments;
- one five-person Group B pool provided `E`, `D`, and `F` judgments across all
  three domains; and
- no individual served in both Group A and Group B.

Group A included raters with relevant psychology and mental-health training,
teachers and university teaching assistants, and raters with relevant medical
training for the corresponding domains. Group B included author-team members
and research interns who were familiar with the framework and contributed to
rubric development. This involvement is disclosed because it may introduce
shared-interpretation or expectancy effects.

## Consent, compensation, and workload

Annotators gave informed consent before participating. Compensation was
provided through existing grant-supported research roles rather than through a
separate per-response payment scheme. Participation was self-paced over two
weeks. Annotators could pause or withdraw at any time; no withdrawal was
reported.

Each Group A rater evaluated the responses in one domain: 660 in mental health
or 415 in education or general health. Each Group B rater evaluated all 1,490
responses on the three diagnostic dimensions.

## Sensitive material and independence

Mental-health materials included potentially distressing and crisis-related
content. The protocol warned raters about sensitive content, allowed breaks,
and instructed them to stop and contact the research lead if participation
caused distress.

Ratings were completed independently. Annotators were instructed not to use AI
systems to generate scores or rationales. They could consult an AI tool only to
clarify material they did not understand; such clarification did not supply the
study judgments.

## Anonymous-review evidence

The signed approval record is retained privately because it identifies the
institution, investigators, and committee members. It can be supplied
confidentially to the chairs if required. Rater names and direct identifiers are
not included in this supplement.
